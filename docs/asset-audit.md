# Shift Work — asset audit (Phase 5)

What every art, UI and audio asset in the game is, where it came from as far
as the repository can tell, and whether it should ship. The goal behind it:
ship with **no AI-made assets**, so the Steam AI-content disclosure can
honestly say "none".

**This is an audit of the repository. It can't prove where a file
originally came from.** It records what the repo shows: commit history,
license files, CREDITS.md entries, file metadata and what the files look
like. Where a source isn't written down anywhere, the row says "unknown".
It doesn't guess.

How to read the **Action** column, following the brief's rule:
- **Keep**: a real, written-down source with a license that allows a
  commercial release.
- **Replace**: anything drawn or generated by code, or of unknown origin,
  unless a real source turns up. "Replace (verify)" means a source may
  exist (the developer added the file); find the receipt or license and the
  row becomes Keep, or replace it.
- Priority: **High** = on screen most of the time or certain to be in the
  trailer; **Medium** = seen every shift, but small or brief; **Low** =
  rarely seen.

Code-drawn art in this project was written in AI-assisted sessions (the
`_draw()` shapes, Polygon2D builders and synthesized sounds were produced by
the coding assistant from text descriptions). Whether that counts as
"AI-generated content" for Steam is the developer's call. Replacing it
settles the question either way, and that's what the brief's rule asks
for.

**A1-A11, the "supermarket" and "warehouse" packs. These need answers
before anything else.** Nothing in the repo records where they came from: no
license file, no readme, no store page, no PNG metadata. The commit message
only names them. Looking at the sheets, there are signs often seen in
AI-generated "pixel art" packs:
- the posters and signs carry garbled pseudo-text ("SAHADE!", "Supernat",
  "FDDD TASTO" on `1.png` and `10.png`);
- most objects don't sit on the 48 px grid the pack claims (Week 13's
  commit already noted "mostly NOT on the 48 px grid");
- the "pixels" vary in size within a sheet.

That isn't proof. It's why the rows say "verify" and not "keep". If the
developer has the purchase receipt or the store page for these packs and it
states the art is hand-made, record it in CREDITS.md and they become Keep.
If not, they're the biggest replacement job in the game: floors, walls,
shelving, every product, the checkouts and the forklift.

## 5. Licensing problems and gaps found

1. **The supermarket and warehouse packs (A1-A11): no source or license at
   all**, plus visual signs consistent with AI generation. This is the
   blocking item for an honest "no AI content" answer, and those packs
   cover most of what's on screen.
2. **Employee of the Month portrait (A14):** origin of the image not
   recorded, and it may depict a real person (the commit calls it the
   "CaseOh photo"). Needs the creator's say-so, and the person's permission
   if it's their likeness, or a replacement.
3. **LPC characters (A16) are CC-BY-SA 3.0.** That's fine for a commercial
   game, but: the finished sprite sheets must be credited (they are:
   in-game Credits and CREDITS.md), and anyone may reuse those sheets under
   the same license, which is the "share-alike" part. It applies to the
   sprite sheets, not to the game's code or other art. If that's
   unacceptable, rebuild the looks from only the OGA-BY/CC0 layers
   (CREDITS-LPC.csv lists each layer's license options). The manager's suit
   coat, formal shirt, necktie and a few hairstyles are the BY-SA/GPL-only
   ones.
4. **Kelano Studio furniture (A12) and karsiori vending machines (A13):**
   CREDITS.md has the license wording but no source URL or saved license.
   Add both, so the terms can be shown if anyone asks.
5. **Godot's own license:** the engine's MIT notice (and its bundled
   font's) must ship with the game; a line in the credits plus the license
   text in a file next to the executable is the usual way.
6. **Unused files (A11, A15, C25, the 47 unused vending machines)** get
   packed into exports unless filtered out. Unused art of unknown origin
   in the build is still "in the build".
7. **No terms seen that forbid resale or AI training** in any written-down
   license (CC0, CC-BY 3.0, CC-BY-SA 3.0, Kelano's free-commercial terms).
   The unknown packs can't be checked.
